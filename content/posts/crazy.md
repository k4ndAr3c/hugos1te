## I want your personal opinion on actual behavior of human kind

Here’s a direct take, without sugarcoating.

Human behavior, taken as a whole, is a mixed and often contradictory package shaped by evolutionary pressures that are only partially updated for the modern world.

What stands out most:

    We are intensely social and status-oriented. Most of what people do—work, politics, culture, social media, even charity—is heavily influenced by the desire for status, belonging, and relative standing. This produces cooperation and impressive collective achievements, but also endless signaling, tribalism, and zero-sum competition over prestige.
    Short-term incentives dominate long-term thinking. Individuals and groups routinely discount the future. This shows up in everything from personal health choices to environmental decisions to fiscal policy. The capacity for delayed gratification exists, but it is unevenly distributed and easily overridden by immediate rewards or social pressure.
    Self-deception is normal, not exceptional. People are good at constructing narratives that protect their self-image and group identity. Evidence that contradicts those narratives is often filtered, rationalized, or attacked. This is not limited to one ideology or culture; it is a general feature of human cognition.
    Empathy and cruelty coexist easily. Most people can be kind and cooperative within their circle and indifferent or harsh outside it. The circle expands or contracts depending on incentives, scarcity, and perceived threat. Large-scale moral progress (reduced violence, expanded rights) has occurred, but it is fragile and often requires strong institutions rather than pure goodwill.
    Technology amplifies whatever is already there. It has not fundamentally rewritten human nature. It has mainly increased the scale and speed at which both constructive and destructive tendencies operate.

In short: humans are capable of remarkable intelligence, creativity, and cooperation, yet remain constrained by tribal instincts, status-seeking, cognitive biases, and weak impulse control at the collective level. Progress happens, but it is uneven, reversible, and usually the product of better incentives and institutions rather than a sudden elevation of human character.

That’s the pattern that seems most consistent across history and current observation. Individuals vary enormously; the aggregate tendencies are more stubborn.
